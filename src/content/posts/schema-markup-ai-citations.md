In February 2024, Gartner predicted that by 2026, traditional search engine volume would drop 25% as AI chatbots become substitute answer engines ([source](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2024-02-19-gartner-predicts-search-engine-volume-will-drop-25-percent-by-2026-due-to-ai-chatbots-and-other-virtual-agents)). That's this year. Schema markup is the answer most agencies will sell you first for AI search visibility — here's what the evidence actually says.

Ahrefs tracked 1,885 pages that added JSON-LD schema between August 2025 and March 2026, compared them against 4,000 control pages, and measured citation changes. The result: essentially no lift. AI Mode moved +2.4%, ChatGPT +2.2% — both statistically meaningless — and AI Overviews declined 4.6% ([source](https://www.seroundtable.com/study-schema-citations-study-41311.html)).

## Why the stat everyone quotes is misleading you

65-71% of pages cited by AI tools do carry structured data ([source](https://www.gogochimp.com/blog/schema-markup-for-ai-seo-2026)). But that's correlation: pages that bother to add schema also tend to already be doing the deeper technical SEO, content depth, and link-building that actually earns the citation ([source](https://elevarus.com/schema-markup-ai-citations-ahrefs-study-may-2026/)). As Ahrefs put it: if you're already doing the rest of the SEO work well, JSON-LD isn't going to be the unlock ([source](https://www.madx.digital/learn/schema-structured-data-for-ai-search)).

## What AI tools actually "see" when they visit your site

A searchVIU test across ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Gemini, and Google's AI Mode found none of five systems could extract a price that only existed in hidden schema code ([source](https://www.madx.digital/learn/schema-structured-data-for-ai-search)). OpenAI's crawler skips roughly 1 in 9 JavaScript-heavy pages, and Anthropic's closer to 1 in 4.

## So should you spend money on this or not?

Keep doing basic schema as routine technical hygiene, but stop paying a premium for it as an "AI SEO" silver bullet. Three real reasons to still have it: it's foundational hygiene for entity disambiguation, it's cheap once automated, and it supports measurement — tracking which pages get cited and correlating that against content and distribution work.

## What to tell an agency pitching "AI schema optimization"

Ask them directly how that claim holds up against the Ahrefs controlled study. A credible answer repositions schema as hygiene, not a growth lever.

## FAQ

**Should we rip out our existing schema markup?** No — keep it as hygiene.

**Is paying extra for "AI schema optimization" worth it?** Generally not as a standalone service.

**Did Google's recent changes affect this?** Somewhat — Google removed FAQ rich results from Search shortly before this study came out.

**What should we spend AI-search budget on instead?** Deeper content, technical SEO fundamentals, and earned links and mentions.

**How do we check our AI crawler access?** A raw HTML check (no JavaScript rendering) shows what a JS-skipping crawler actually sees.
